UK or Canada? Choosing where to study as a Nigerian student

Course length, costs, visas and work after graduation: the differences that matter when you are choosing between the two.

By the Neo Consult team·23 September 2026·5 min read

The UK and Canada are two of the most popular destinations for Nigerian students. Both offer excellent universities, but they suit different plans. Here are the differences that matter most.

Course length

Most taught master’s degrees in the UK take one year, which saves a year of tuition and living costs. In Canada, master’s programmes usually take one to two years and often include co-op work placements. Undergraduate degrees are typically three years in England and four in Canada.

Cost

Tuition varies widely by university and subject in both countries, so compare the total cost of your specific course, including living costs in that city. A shorter UK course can work out cheaper overall even when the yearly fees look similar.

Getting your visa or study permit

For the UK you’ll need a CAS and proof of funds for the Student visa (see our visa checklist). Canada introduced a cap on study permits in 2024, and many applicants now need a provincial attestation letter from the province where they’ll study. Requirements are changing often, so check the latest rules on the official Government of Canada website.

Working after you graduate

The UK’s Graduate route lets eligible students stay and work after completing a degree. Canada’s Post-Graduation Work Permit can last up to three years depending on your programme, and Canada has well-known routes to permanent residency. Check current eligibility rules for both before you decide, as they’re reviewed regularly.

Which one is right for you?

  • Choose the UK if you want a shorter, cheaper master’s and access to world-ranked universities, with support nearby from our Leeds team.
  • Choose Canada if you’re planning for the long term, value co-op work experience, or want to explore routes to settling permanently.
